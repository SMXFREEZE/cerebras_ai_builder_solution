This is AssetOps, a scanner-first asset tracking system for Cerebras manufacturing. This video is generated from the live local app after a data reset, so the scans, errors, writebacks, filters, and audit pages are being exercised, not mocked.

The home screen gives the reviewer the product context: operations, facilities, and finance all hold part of the truth, and one missed scan creates drift. The technician console has the four hot-path workflows: receive, store, deploy, and transfer. Each flow names exactly which systems it writes.

Receive starts at the dock. The serial, model, class, and receiving location are visible before the tag scan. I scan a fresh asset and the app accepts it. I scan the same tag again to prove duplicate receives are idempotent. Then I change the serial and scan again; the conflict shows the expected serial and the scanned serial, so recovery is specific.

Store is two-step on purpose. The asset tag is scanned first, the app previews the current asset, and only then does the technician scan the shelf. One extra read is cheaper than putting expensive hardware in the wrong place.

Deploy is stricter because it creates downstream side effects. A rack without an RU is rejected before anything is written. A complete rack commits the operations state, writes the facilities rack assignment, and capitalizes the asset in finance.

Transfer is custody only. The from user is the logged-in technician. The receiving badge is explicit. The app rejects transferring to yourself, then accepts a real handoff while keeping the asset in service.

I also show de-racking. When an in-service asset is stored, operations moves it to stored and facilities is cleared, while finance stays untouched.

The manager dashboard is the standup view: first exception, critical and review counts, clean inventory, search, and filters. I search for the scanned asset and open its detail page. The detail page shows current placement, procurement context, and the event log for receive, store, deploy, and transfer.

The reconciliation page joins operations, facilities, and finance server-side, then ranks differences as fix today, needs a human, probably fine, or clean. The barcode sheet is the reviewer kit: happy paths plus duplicate, disposed, drifted, ghost, location, incomplete rack, and badge cases.
